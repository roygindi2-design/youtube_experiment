**Pattern:** `lesson_consequence` · **Opening:** `spaghetti_slurped_up_the_trunk` · **World:** sunny garden patio for lunch: a little round table with a red-and-white checkered tablecloth under a yellow striped umbrella, lemon trees in big terracotta pots, a white wall with climbing flowers

## Story beats

1. Tito sucks a long spaghetti strand up his trunk like a straw, slurp! Red sauce flies everywhere (funny problem, second 0)
2. Extreme close-up: sauce on his face, his ears and his overalls, spaghetti on his head (consequence)
3. Mamá Elefanta gently wipes his face with a napkin (mentor, no scolding)
4. Top-down on the plate: Mamá's trick, the little fork turns and turns and makes a neat spaghetti ball (the trick)
5. Close-up: Tito takes a neat bite from the fork, mouth closed, he smiles (he learns)
6. Tito proudly holds up his clean empty plate (reward)
7. Tito and Mamá eat together neatly at the checkered table, both with forks (moral)
8. One last spaghetti strand on the plate: Tito looks around, winks, and slurps it up his trunk (loop gag)

## Spanish check (Mexico, ages 1–4)

- **espagueti** (Mexican spelling), **trompa** (elephant trunk, used across Latin America), **carita** / **limpiecito** — warm Mexican diminutives.
- **Mamá le limpia la carita** — correct indirect object; **Gira y gira el tenedor** describes the fork turning.
- Sound words ¡slurp! / ¡ñam! repeat at start and end (loop).
